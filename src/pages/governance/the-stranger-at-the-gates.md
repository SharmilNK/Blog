The Kingdom of Aurelia had never needed magic.

Its farms flourished. Its merchants prospered. Its scholars solved problems with careful thought and trusted traditions. Every bridge, every law, every harvest had been built through years of discipline.

The kingdom wasn't perfect, but it was prosperous.

One quiet morning, the castle guards noticed something unusual.

A caravan had arrived outside the city gates with a small group of travelers carrying strange glowing devices and books filled with symbols no one could read.

Their leader stepped forward.

"My name is *AI*," she said with a respectful bow.

"We come seeking refuge."

Word spread through the city faster than wildfire.

Some citizens were excited.

"They say *AI* can solve problems faster than any scholar."

"It can write."

"It can paint."

"It can even help doctors."

Others weren't convinced.

"My grandfather said the same thing about the last invention."

"What if they replace our craftsmen?"

"What if they make mistakes?"

"What if they're pretending to help us?"

By sunset, everyone in the kingdom had an opinion.

Only one person's opinion truly mattered. *Queen Leela*.

She stood silently at the castle window, watching the strangers below. Finally, she turned to her captain.

"Bring them food."

The captain looked surprised.

"So... we welcome them?"

"No," the Queen shook her head.

"We observe."

After a long pause, she added,

"History is full of kingdoms destroyed not by armies...

...but by gifts they accepted too quickly."

The room fell silent.

They knew stories of cities that had welcomed beautiful wooden horses through their gates.

They celebrated all night.

By morning...

their kingdom no longer existed.

"What if this is another Trojan Horse?" she wondered.

The next morning, the royal council was summoned. The Queen looked around the council chamber.

"My lords and ladies," she began, "our kingdom has prospered for centuries without *AI*. If we choose to welcome them, how do we ensure it serves our people and not the other way around?"

The Royal Product Advisor (**Product Requirements Document**) was the first to rise.

"Your Majesty, before we decide how *AI* should enter the kingdom, we must first decide why. What problem are we asking it to solve? Who will benefit from it? More importantly, where should its powers end? If we cannot answer those questions, we have no business opening our gates."

The Queen nodded slowly.

"A fair beginning. Purpose before power."

The Royal Architect (**Design**) stepped forward.

"Once we know its purpose, we must think about our people. How will they interact with *AI*? Will they understand its advice? What happens if it is uncertain, makes a mistake, or simply doesn't know the answer? A kingdom loses trust when its people are confused."

The Queen smiled.

"So we must design not just for success, but for failure as well."

Next came the Chief Engineer (**HLD / Eng.md**), carrying a large scroll.

"Majesty, even the finest ideas fail without strong foundations. We must decide where *AI* will live, what knowledge it may access, how it will communicate with our systems, and how all the pieces fit together. A kingdom cannot be built on wishes alone."

The Queen leaned forward.

"So before we build, we design the roads."

Before the engineer could sit, one of the senior council members (**Review**) interrupted.

"I request a review."

The room fell silent.

"What concerns you?" asked the Queen.

"We have spoken of grand ideas, but have we challenged them? Have we considered the risks? Are the architect's plans realistic? Does the engineer's design truly support the advisor's vision? Better we argue here than discover our mistakes after the gates are opened."

The Queen struck the floor with her staff.

"Agreed. A plan that cannot survive questions will never survive reality."

After the council refined their plans, the Master Builder (**LLD / Spec.md**) entered carrying stacks of parchment.

"Your Majesty, the vision is clear. Now every stone must know where it belongs. Every road, every instruction, every safeguard, every test must be written precisely. If our builders each imagine a different kingdom, we shall build chaos instead."

The Queen examined the blueprints.

"So these are no longer ideas. They are instructions."

Another elder council member (**Review**) stood once more.

"One final review, Your Majesty."

The Queen raised an eyebrow.

"Another?"

The elder smiled.

"It is cheaper to erase ink than rebuild castles. Let us inspect every detail before the first stone is laid."

The chamber filled with discussion. Tiny flaws were corrected. Missing details were added. Hidden risks were uncovered. Only when everyone agreed did the Queen look toward the Master Builder.

"You may begin."

Soon, the kingdom's craftsmen (**Implement**) got to work. Carpenters, masons, blacksmiths, scholars, and engineers worked side by side, carefully following the plans the council had approved. Piece by piece, the kingdom prepared a place for *AI*.

Weeks later, the final report arrived.

"The work is complete, Your Majesty."

The great gates opened. *AI* entered the kingdom not as a ruler, nor as a stranger, but as a trusted guest.

The Queen watched carefully, their kingdom had learned how to govern it wisely.

The path they followed, from first question to open gates:

**PRD** → **Design** → **HLD / Eng.md** → **Review** → **LLD / Spec.md** → **Review** → **Implement** → **Deployment**

## Terminology

**PRD (Product Requirements Document)**: defines what problem a product solves, who it serves, and where its scope ends, before any building starts.

**Design**: decides how people will interact with the system, including how it behaves when it is uncertain, wrong, or unable to answer.

**HLD (High Level Design)**: the overall architecture, covering where the system lives, what data it can access, and how the parts communicate.

**Review**: a deliberate checkpoint to challenge assumptions and catch flaws in a plan while fixes are still cheap.

**LLD (Low Level Design)**: the detailed specification where every component, instruction, safeguard, and test is written down so builders share one plan.

**Implement**: the build phase where engineers follow the approved specs to construct the system.

**Deployment**: releasing the finished, reviewed system into production for real use.
